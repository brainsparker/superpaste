How it works

From blank field to first draft.

No copy-pasting, no app switching, no explaining context. Just press a key.

1

Place your cursor

Click into any text field — an email reply, a Slack message, a form, a code file. Anywhere you'd normally type.

2

Press Option V

SuperPaste takes a single screenshot of your active window and sends it to your chosen AI provider over an encrypted connection. A subtle indicator shows it's working.

3

Text appears

The response is pasted directly into your field — no ⌘V required. Read it before sending. Don't like it? ⌘Z undoes it.

SuperPaste vs. Writing Tools

Why not just use Apple Intelligence?

Writing Tools is for editing. SuperPaste is for the blank field before it.

Apple's Writing Tools

Rewrites text you've already written and selected — proofread it, shorten it, change the tone. But an empty reply box has nothing to select, so you still have to type the first draft yourself.

SuperPaste

Composes from an empty cursor using what's already on your screen — the email you're replying to, the question in the form, the thread above the box. Press ⌥V and the draft appears.

FAQ

Fair questions.

Why does it need Screen Recording and Accessibility permissions?

Screen Recording is the macOS permission for capturing the active window — one screenshot, only when you press ⌥V. Accessibility lets SuperPaste register the global hotkey and paste at your cursor. It never records video and never reads your keystrokes.

macOS says SuperPaste "can record your screen and audio." Should I worry?

No — that's a standard reminder macOS shows monthly for every app with Screen Recording permission (screenshot tools, screen recorders, video-call apps), and developers can't turn it off. SuperPaste captures a single frame of a single window, only at the moment you press ⌥V. It never records audio or video, and the code is open source if you'd like to verify.

Is my screen data stored anywhere?

SuperPaste never stores, logs, or trains on your screenshots or responses. Each screenshot is sent over an encrypted connection, used to generate one response, and discarded. Your provider processes it under their API data policies. See each provider's data policy for details. Full details in the privacy policy.

Which apps does it work in?

Anywhere you can place a cursor and ⌘V works: Mail, Slack, every browser, IDEs, Notes — native and Electron apps alike.

What if the output is wrong?

Nothing is sent or submitted — text is just pasted at your cursor. Read it, edit it, or press ⌘Z and it's gone. You're always the last step.

Does it work offline?

No — it needs an AI provider to generate the response. Offline, pressing ⌥V simply does nothing; you'll see a quiet error and your screen goes nowhere.

What happens after the 7-day trial?

Which AI providers does SuperPaste support?

SuperPaste works with any provider you bring. Use the hosted SuperPaste plan (powered by Claude), bring your own Anthropic, OpenAI, or OpenRouter key, or point it at any OpenAI-compatible endpoint - including local models via Ollama, LM Studio, or vLLM. Switch freely in Settings without losing your stored keys.

Is it really open source?

Yes — MIT licensed, with the full source at github.com/brainsparker/superpaste, including both the app and the server.
